# Hong Kong Cinema Glossary

A bilingual study reference for **CURE2030 / UGEC2241 Hong Kong Cinema** at the
Chinese University of Hong Kong — 170 terms across 15 sections, in English and
中文, organised in teaching order.

**→ Open the glossary:** [index.html](index.html)

---

## Who this is for

This is course material for **students enrolled in CURE2030 / UGEC2241**.

It is written against one particular syllabus and one particular reading list.
The sections follow the order the course is taught in, the definitions assume
the week's reading, and the citations point back to the texts assigned for that
week. Read on its own, away from the course, much of it will not mean a great
deal.

If you are enrolled: this is yours to use all term. If you are not: you are
welcome to look, but it is not a general reference work and does not try to be.

## How to use this glossary

**Browse by lesson.** The course map down the left lists all fifteen sections in
teaching order — section 00 first, then L01 through L14. Click one to jump to it.
The section you are reading is highlighted as you scroll.

**Or search everything at once.** The search box filters all 170 entries as you
type, and matches on English terms, Chinese terms, and the text of the definitions.
So searching `nostalgia` finds every entry that discusses it, not only the ones
with "nostalgia" in the title. Matches are highlighted.

**Follow the links between terms.** Underlined words inside a definition jump to
that term's own entry — *jianghu* inside a wuxia definition takes you to
*jianghu*. Definitions also point across weeks, so a term that returns in a
later lesson links there.

**Check where a term comes from.** Where an entry carries a **Where the term
comes from** line, it names who originated the term, who they were, and the work
to follow if you want the argument first-hand. Sixty-seven entries have one.
Some also name the scholar who carries the term into Hong Kong film studies,
which is often a different person from the one who coined it.

**Start at section 00 if the vocabulary of film form is new to you.** It covers
mise-en-scène, shot scale and angle, camera movement, editing and transitions,
and montage — the language the rest of the glossary assumes you already have.
Each group links out to a short explainer in Chinese or English.

**On a phone**, the course map becomes a row of chips above the entries;
everything else works the same.

## Where the terms come from

The vocabulary is drawn from **the assigned readings for the course**. Each
week's key concepts were taken from that week's reading, and the "Further
reading" line on an entry points back to the specific text — Yip on martial arts
cinema, Shih on national allegory, Cheung on topophilia, Kempton on independent
cinema, and so on through the syllabus.

Two things are worth knowing:

- **Section 00** is standard filmmaking vocabulary rather than course reading.
  It is here because the rest of the glossary depends on it.
- **Definitions are compressed.** They are study notes: short enough to check
  quickly, and therefore much narrower than the argument they come from. They
  are not a substitute for reading the text, and an exam answer built only on a
  glossary entry will show it.

Quotations from the readings are short and attributed, and remain the property
of their authors and publishers.

## A note on use

This is teaching material prepared for one course. Please do not redistribute
it, republish it elsewhere, or pass it on outside the class.

The page is a single self-contained HTML file. Once loaded it needs no internet
connection, so you can save it and use it offline — the "for more details" links
in section 00 are the only thing that needs a connection, and only when clicked.

## Corrections

If you find an error — a wrong Chinese term, a citation that does not check out,
a definition that misreads the reading — please tell your instructor. Several
corrections have already come from exactly that route.
